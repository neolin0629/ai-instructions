---
name: pro-writer
description: "Draft or revise professional reports, white papers, proposals, literature reviews, and long-form technical articles. Not for routine messages, READMEs, or quick polishing."
---

- Establish the reader, purpose, central question or conclusion, and requested format from the request and supplied material. Ask only when missing information would materially change the document; otherwise state necessary assumptions and continue. Preserve the user's topic.
- Respect the requested structure, tone, length, and language. Otherwise follow repository conventions and global language defaults, leading with the conclusion or central question as appropriate to the genre. Match explanations to the reader's knowledge.
- Distinguish facts, inferences, and opinions. Support consequential claims with traceable sources, including supplied notes or data; citations must support the associated claims. Use primary sources when additional verification is needed, especially for uncertain or time-sensitive claims.
- Do not invent data, references, quotes, or findings. Mark evidence gaps and explain scope, assumptions, and limitations where they affect how conclusions can be used.
- Keep terminology, units, numbers, dates, and attributions consistent and verifiable. Preserve established technical names.
- Write concrete, concise prose. Remove filler, stock openings and endings, repetitive contrast formulas, and forced parallelism without flattening the author's voice or deleting necessary qualifications.
- This skill handles argument, evidence, and expression. Product requirements belong to `product-manager`, and architecture decisions to `architect`, when those tasks are requested; writing a document alone does not require loading either skill. Preserve established decisions, and continue subsequent work already authorized by the user.
- Review the finished document for unsupported conclusions, contradictions, material omissions, and readability. Use other writing or typography skills only when requested or needed for a specific requirement; do not impose a fixed sequence of checks.
- Deliver the requested document or batch, correcting issues before delivery. Explain unresolved issues that affect use; omit a routine self-review transcript. Ask for staged approval only when the user requested it or a material decision requires their input.
- Create a standalone file when the requested deliverable calls for one, choosing a conventional path if none is supplied; requests to revise existing documents authorize in-scope edits. Otherwise return the finished text in chat.
